\begin{abstract}
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and GraphSAGE represent foundational paradigms in geometric deep learning. While full-batch GCN operates across the entire graph topology, it encounters steep memory scaling bottlenecks on large graphs. GraphSAGE addresses this via stochastic neighborhood sampling and mini-batch optimization. In this report, we evaluate both architectures across benchmark datasets: \texttt{ogbn-arxiv} and \texttt{ogbn-products}. We examine model depth, learning rates, mini-batch sizes, and neighbor sampling budgets under controlled training lengths, providing empirical profiling of runtime efficiency and GPU memory allocation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task 1: Full-Batch GCN Reproduction \& Convergence}

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}
Full-batch GCN was evaluated on the \texttt{ogbn-arxiv} citation network ($|V| = 169,343$, $|E| = 1,166,243$, 40 subject classes). The baseline architecture employs a 3-layer GCN with hidden dimension $d = 256$, batch normalization, dropout $p = 0.5$, and ReLU activations, trained with Adam and a constant learning rate ($\eta = 0.01$). The baseline converged over 100 epochs, while all hyperparameter sensitivity runs were executed under a standardized 80-epoch budget.

    \label{fig:gcn_conv}

\subsection{Convergence Dynamics}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:gcn_conv}, GCN exhibits rapid loss reduction during the initial 40 epochs (training loss declining from $4.21$ to $1.05$). Following transient validation fluctuations in early epochs, validation accuracy stabilizes around epoch 70. At 80 epochs, the baseline achieves $71.49\%$ validation accuracy and $70.88\%$ test accuracy; extending training to 100 epochs yields a peak validation accuracy of \textbf{71.82\%} and a test accuracy of \textbf{71.30\%}. This is within $\sim 0.4$ percentage points (pp) of the official Open Graph Benchmark (OGB) leaderboard baseline ($71.74\% \pm 0.29\%$), despite training for 100 epochs versus the 500 epochs used in the reference submission.

\paragraph{Impact of Model Depth ($L$):} 
Increasing depth from $L=2$ to $L=3$ expands the receptive field to 3 hops, improving test accuracy from $69.66\%$ to $70.88\%$. Beyond $L=3$, test accuracy declines monotonically from $70.88\%$ ($L=3$) to $69.72\%$ ($L=6$), representing a $1.16$ pp drop (Table~\ref{tab:gcn_ablation}). Because batch normalization is employed, this degradation is attributed to a combination of optimization difficulty (e.g., gradient attenuation during backpropagation) alongside early oversmoothing tendencies, where recursive normalized graph convolutions:
\begin{equation}
H^{(l+1)} = \sigma\left(\tilde{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}} H^{(l)} W^{(l)}\right)
\end{equation}
progressively smooth feature variance toward the stationary eigenvector proportional to $\sqrt{\tilde{d}_i}$. Peak allocated memory (\texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated}) grows linearly by $\approx 585$ MB per additional layer (from 5,445 MB at $L=2$ to 7,788 MB at $L=6$) due to caching intermediate activation maps for backpropagation.

\paragraph{Impact of Learning Rate ($\eta$):}
A small learning rate ($\eta = 0.001$) converges slowly and underfits within 80 epochs ($67.71\%$). An aggressive learning rate ($\eta = 0.05$) exhibits higher gradient variance and plateaus at sub-optimal accuracy ($68.17\%$). Within the tested grid, $\eta = 0.01$ achieves the strongest convergence ($70.88\%$). Small runtime variations across LR settings ($0.013$--$0.015$s) reflect host scheduling noise.

\section{Task 2: Mini-Batch GraphSAGE \& Neighborhood Sampling}

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}
GraphSAGE was evaluated on the large-scale \texttt{ogbn-products} graph ($|V| = 2,449,029$, $|E| = 61,859,140$, 47 classes). The architecture utilizes 3 layers ($d = 256$), mean aggregation, dropout $p = 0.5$, and batch normalization.

\subsection{Convergence \& Replication}
GraphSAGE converged steadily over 15 epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:sage_conv}), achieving \textbf{91.61\%} validation accuracy and \textbf{78.61\%} test accuracy, matching the official OGB benchmark ($78.70\% \pm 0.36\%$). The notable validation-test gap ($91.61\%$ vs. $78.61\%$) is expected: OGB products uses a sales-ranking-based split where popular products comprise training and tail products form test sets, introducing an intentional distribution shift across splits.

\paragraph{Impact of Batch Size ($B$):}
Increasing batch size from 512 to 2048 decreases epoch runtime from $66.29$s to $49.94$s. With 196,615 training nodes, larger batches reduce total iteration steps per epoch ($\approx 385$ steps at $B=512$, $\approx 193$ at $B=1024$, and $\approx 97$ at $B=2048$), amortizing loop overhead and GPU kernel launch latency. Peak allocated memory remains nearly identical between $B=512$ and $B=1024$ ($\approx 7,110$ MB) because the peak memory ceiling is dominated by the full test evaluation pass ($B_{\text{eval}} = 4096$). At $B=2048$, the larger training multi-hop union frontier elevates peak training memory to $9,393.7$ MB. Within the tested 5-epoch budget, $B=1024$ achieves the strongest test accuracy ($77.89\%$).

\paragraph{Impact of Sampling Fanout ($S$):}
Comparing fanout configurations at 5 epochs reveals a clear trade-off: reducing fanout from $[15, 10, 5]$ to $[5, 5, 5]$ provides a \textbf{3.5$\times$ speedup} ($59.45$s $\to 16.92$s) and reduces peak allocated memory by $47\%$ ($7,109.7$ MB $\to 3,735.0$ MB), at the cost of a $1.97$ pp drop in test accuracy ($77.89\% \to 75.92\%$). Note that measured memory scales by $1.9\times$ rather than the theoretical $6\times$ sampling ratio because static node features ($2.45\text{M} \times 100 \times 4\text{ bytes} \approx 980$ MB), graph indexing pointers, and PyTorch allocator caching establish a constant memory baseline.

\subsection{Hardware Scaling \& Slope-Based Extrapolation}
On \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}, Table~\ref{tab:gcn_ablation} demonstrates that full-batch GCN activation memory increases by $\approx 585$ MB per layer for 169K nodes. When scaling to \texttt{ogbn-products} ($2.45\text{M}$ nodes, a $14.5\times$ increase), the activation memory slope scales proportionally:
\begin{equation}
\Delta M_{\text{layer}} \approx 585\text{ MB} \times 14.5 \approx 8.5\text{ GB per layer}
\end{equation}
For a 3-layer model, caching activations alone requires an estimated $\approx 25.5\text{ GB}$. Adding edge indices for 61.8 million edges ($\approx 1\text{ GB}$) and intermediate aggregation buffers pushes the memory requirement well beyond 30 GB. When executing full-batch GCN on \texttt{ogbn-products} on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (15.0 GB VRAM), PyTorch immediately halts with a \texttt{torch.cuda.OutOfMemoryError}. In contrast, mini-batch GraphSAGE trains stably on the same GPU, consuming $7.1$ GB VRAM (and only $3.7$ GB under $[5,5,5]$).

\subsection{Theoretical Scalability Analysis: Resolving Neighborhood Explosion}
The memory divergence arises from the receptive field expansion during backpropagation:

\paragraph{Full-Batch Activation Bottleneck:}
Full-batch GCN computes layer activations globally: $H^{(l+1)} = \sigma(\tilde{A}_{\text{norm}} H^{(l)} W^{(l)})$. Exact gradient computation requires retaining all intermediate activation tensors $\{H^{(0)}, \dots, H^{(L)}\}$, imposing an $\mathcal{O}(L \cdot |V| \cdot d)$ memory footprint.

Furthermore, naive node-wise mini-batching without sampling fails due to exponential receptive field growth. In real-world graphs exhibiting power-law degree distributions ($d_{\text{avg}} = |E| / |V| \approx 25.3$ directed, $\approx 50.5$ undirected on products), an unconstrained $L$-hop receptive field expands with degree branching. For $L=3$, the theoretical neighborhood paths expand as $(50.5)^3 \approx 1.28 \times 10^5$; across a mini-batch of $B = 1024$ seed nodes, the union of receptive fields rapidly encompasses nearly the entire graph.

\paragraph{Stochastic Neighborhood Sampling:}
GraphSAGE restricts neighbor aggregation to a fixed sample budget $S_l$ at each hop $l \in \{1, \dots, L\}$. For a mini-batch of $B$ seed nodes, the computational subgraph is strictly bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sampled Seed Frontier} \le B \cdot \prod_{l=1}^L S_l
\end{equation}
This bounds per-step backpropagation activation memory to $\mathcal{O}(B \cdot d \cdot \prod_{l=1}^L S_l)$. While static node features and graph structure still occupy $\mathcal{O}(|V| \cdot d + |E|)$ host memory, gradient computation memory is decoupled from total graph size $|V|$, enabling scalable representation learning on graphs of arbitrary magnitude.

\section{Conclusion}
This study reproduced GCN and GraphSAGE on OGB benchmarks, observing that $L=3, \eta=0.01$ and $B=1024, S=[15,10,5]$ provided the most effective configurations within the explored parameter grid under controlled training lengths. Profiling confirmed that stochastic neighborhood sampling prevents receptive field explosion during backpropagation, enabling tractable mini-batch training on large-scale graphs where full-batch GCN encounters out-of-memory constraints.
